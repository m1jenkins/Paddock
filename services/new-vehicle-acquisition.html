New exotics, ordered and bought for you

A new GT3, 296, or specced Aston Martin rarely sits on a dealer’s lot. It comes through an allocation or a factory order, often with a waitlist and a markup. We find the route to your car, negotiate the price, and handle the purchase.

Start an acquisition How it works

Walking into a dealership and driving home the same day is not how these cars get bought.

Manufacturers allocate high-demand models to their dealers in small numbers, based on each dealer’s sales history with the brand. The dealer then decides which customers get them, and people who have bought there before usually come first. On the most wanted cars, the dealer can also add a market adjustment that runs tens of thousands over MSRP.

We use dealer relationships across Texas and elsewhere in the US to get you an allocation or a factory order, and we negotiate the price on your side of the table.

Four ways to the car

After the first conversation, we tell you which of these fits your car, your spec, and your timeline.

Factory order

You choose the spec and a dealer places the order with the factory. We go through the configurator with you first: paint, interior, performance packages, and options.

Allocation transfer

Another dealer holds an allocation for the car and is willing to release it. We find that dealer and arrange the purchase.

Dealer locate

The car is already built and sitting with a dealer somewhere in the country. We find it and negotiate the price.

Waitlist

When there’s no faster route, we put you on the right dealers’ lists and stay in touch with them until a car comes up.

Questions

How do Porsche allocations work in Texas?

Porsche Cars North America allocates new cars to authorized dealers based on each dealer’s sales history and relationship with Porsche. The dealer then chooses which customers get them. High-demand models like the GT3, GT3 RS, and 911 S/T have the strictest criteria. We use our dealer relationships to get you an allocation faster and at a fair price.

Can you get me a new car without a dealer markup?

Often. Markups, which dealers call market adjustments, are common on high-demand models. We use our dealer relationships to negotiate MSRP or close to it wherever a dealer will sell at that price.

Do I need a purchase history with the dealer?

For some brands it matters a lot, Ferrari especially. We can introduce you to dealers and work out a route to your first allocation even if you haven’t bought from them before. Our Ferrari allocation guide explains how that system works.

What does it cost?

The first consultation is free. After that, fees are set per engagement. There’s no published rate card.

Do you only buy from Texas dealers?

No. Our clients are in Texas, but we work with dealers across the state and elsewhere in the US.
